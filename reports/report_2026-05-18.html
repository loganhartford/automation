The physical product is a bosonic qubit module built from an aluminum superconducting cavity containing two internal posts, each supporting a distinct resonant mode. These multi-mode bosonic qubits encode quantum information using multiple microwave frequencies within a single qubit, which enables the intrinsic correction of multiple error types — including bit flips, phase flips, and control errors — during computation. The approach involves manipulating microwaves in a supercooled cavity, meaning the company does not require additional hardware to handle error correction. The core problem Nord Quantique is attacking is the overhead cost of quantum error correction. Conventional approaches can require devoting 100 to 1,000 physical qubits to keeping just one single qubit functioning correctly. Nord Quantique's superconducting architecture uses bosonic error-correcting codes to reach a 1:1 logical-to-physical qubit ratio, correcting errors intrinsically at the hardware level, which dramatically reduces system size, complexity, and energy requirements. This is intended to allow the company to build machines that fit into existing data centres , rather than requiring large industrial facilities. In early 2024, the company demonstrated quantum error correction on a single superconducting qubit that increased the qubit's coherence time by 14% without using any additional physical qubits — creating a logical qubit from a single physical quantum mode — marking the first time a company had extended a qubit's lifetime via active error correction at the individual qubit level.
